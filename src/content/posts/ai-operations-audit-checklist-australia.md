An AI operations audit should give you enough evidence to decide whether to change a process and what to change first. Start with one team and a recurring task it can demonstrate, such as transferring quote details to a CRM or compiling a weekly report.

## Bring examples the team can discuss safely

Prepare two or three redacted examples. Include an ordinary run, an exception and a failed or delayed outcome. Remove customer records, access tokens and confidential attachments before sharing them. Name each tool and the person who manages it. Confirm which plan your team uses because integration access can vary by plan.

The Australian Cyber Security Centre recommends defining which information staff can upload into AI tools. Agree those rules before any discovery sample enters a cloud service. [ACSC small business AI guide](https://www.cyber.gov.au/publication/artificial-intelligence-for-small-business)

## Describe the work in numbers and owners

- **How often does this run?**. A representative week or month and any seasonal peaks.
- **How long does it take?**. Normal handling time plus time spent correcting exceptions.
- **Who decides what happens next?**. One named owner and someone who can cover absences.
- **What can go wrong?**. Examples of late, duplicate, missing or unauthorised updates.
- **How would you recognise an improvement?**. One baseline measure such as fewer manual re-entries or faster review.

Involve someone who does the work and someone who can approve a change. Ask them to identify steps that depend on judgement, such as resolving a mismatched quote or authorising an invoice. A mapped approval point is easier to estimate and test than a general instruction to “keep a human in the loop”.

## Check what the audit will hand over

Ask for a current workflow map, a proposed flow and the integration constraints. Look for an estimate with explicit assumptions and a clear first build or a decision to keep the current process. Clarify who will own accounts, workflow documentation and maintenance if you subsequently approve implementation.
