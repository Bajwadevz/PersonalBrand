A useful quote starts with a task someone can demonstrate. “Connect the enquiry inbox to our CRM and route exceptions to the sales coordinator” gives a team something to price and test. “Put AI into sales” leaves both the work and the budget open.

## Why two similar requests can cost different amounts

Consider an enquiry that needs to become a CRM record. A form with a required email and one assigned owner has a short path. A shared inbox may contain attachments, replies from existing customers and requests for several branches. Someone must decide how to match those inputs, what to do with duplicates and when to stop for review. Watch the [lead-response walkthrough](/work#workflow) for an introduction to one way enquiries can move towards a booking.

Check the subscription plan as well. A connector that works in a developer account may need a different plan or permission in production. Ask for those dependencies and their ongoing cost in the quote.

## Put a number on the work first

Suppose a team handles 120 enquiries a month and spends four minutes copying each one. That is eight hours of copying. If 15 enquiries still need four minutes of review, that leaves seven hours before monitoring, corrections and maintenance. These are illustrative inputs. Measure a normal week in your own team before relying on an estimate.

Released time may help a coordinator follow up open quotes. Cash savings require an actual change in spend, such as reduced overtime. If the workflow is infrequent or the CRM already has a suitable native rule, configuring that rule may be the most economical decision.

## What to bring to discovery

Bring one redacted example, the names of the systems involved, a rough monthly count and the person who handles exceptions. Explain which action needs a human sign-off. With that information, a [discovery call](/contact) can establish whether a paid investigation makes sense.
