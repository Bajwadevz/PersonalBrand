If you run a 10-person business in Perth or regional WA, the AI advice you see is probably split between free tool tutorials and agencies offering to "transform" your operations. Most owners need something in between. They want to know which job to start with, whether a free tool will do and when paying someone actually saves money.

This guide starts with the free and low-cost help available to WA businesses, then explains when outside support makes sense. We are an implementation agency, so we have an interest in that last part. That is also why we recommend starting with the free options.

## Free and low-cost help available to WA businesses

Check each program's page before you plan around it. Workshop dates and grant rounds change, and the details below were checked in November 2026.

**Small Business Development Corporation (SBDC).** The WA Government's SBDC offers [free business advice](https://www.smallbusiness.wa.gov.au/business-advisory) to WA small business owners by phone, email, live chat or appointment, with advisers across regional WA. It has also published a plain-English guide, [Using AI in your business](https://www.smallbusiness.wa.gov.au/templates-tools-guides/guides/using-ai-in-your-business), which covers choosing tools by risk, checking outputs and protecting customer information. The SBDC runs workshops too. Its [Level Up: AI for Small Business](https://www.smallbusiness.wa.gov.au/workshops/level-ai-small-business) series ran earlier in 2026, so check the [workshop calendar](https://www.smallbusiness.wa.gov.au/workshops) for future sessions.

**National AI Centre events and guidance.** The federal National AI Centre publishes [practical guidance on preparing your business for AI](https://www.ai.gov.au/planning-ai/prepare-your-business) and lists events nationally. These have included WA sessions such as the [AI for WA SMEs: Practical Workflow Clinic](https://www.ai.gov.au/news-and-insights/events/ai-wa-smes-practical-workflow-clinic), hosted by the Western Australian AI Hub. That clinic is a paid, in-person session focused on choosing one low-risk workflow. Confirm the date, time and price on the listing before booking.

**AI Adopt Centres.** The federal government funds a small number of [AI Adopt Centres](https://business.gov.au/expertise-and-advice/ai-adopt-centres) that offer free training, consultations, demonstrations and safety resources to eligible SMEs. Most have a sector focus, such as manufacturing, agriculture or regional industries, and the page notes eligibility criteria. One of them, the Safe AI Adoption Model (SAAM), offers free online AI safety resources that any business can read.

## What about grants?

Grants come up in almost every conversation about AI in WA. The honest answer is that most of them are narrower than owners expect.

- **Innovation Booster Grant (WA)**. The [Innovation Booster Grant](https://www.wa.gov.au/organisation/department-of-energy-and-economic-diversification/new-industries-and-innovation-fund-innovation-booster-grant) supports early-stage founders, startups and innovation-driven SMEs developing something new. Its 2026 round has already been awarded. Read the guidelines carefully, because the grant is designed for innovation projects rather than everyday operating costs. Automating your existing invoicing is unlikely to qualify.
- **Local Capability Fund – Digital Transformation round (WA)**. This round has funded WA SMEs adopting digital technologies. The [key rounds page](https://www.wa.gov.au/organisation/department-of-energy-and-economic-diversification/local-capability-fund-key-rounds-and-dates) shows the last rounds closed in September 2025 and says the fund is under review. Check the page for any new round before budgeting around it.
- **Federal grants**. Search [business.gov.au grants and programs](https://business.gov.au/grants-and-programs) by state and industry for anything current.

Be cautious of any provider who builds a proposal around a grant you have not yet been awarded. Plan the project so it still makes sense without the grant.

## A DIY-first path

Before paying anyone, including us, try this sequence.

1. **Pick one repetitive job.** Good candidates are tasks that happen often, follow a pattern and annoy the person doing them. Examples include copying web enquiries into your CRM, retyping approved quotes into Xero or chasing the same missing information from customers.
2. **Measure it for one normal week.** Count how many times it happens and roughly how long each takes. Without this, you cannot tell whether any fix was worth it.
3. **Check what your current tools already do.** HubSpot, Xero, MYOB and most job management tools have built-in automations or marketplace apps. A setting you already pay for often beats a new build.
4. **Use the free help.** Take the job to an SBDC adviser or a workshop and ask whether it is a sensible first step.
5. **Try a small, reversible fix.** A form with required fields, an email template or a simple no-code automation can remove a surprising amount of manual work. Keep a person checking results until you trust them.

Many WA teams will stop here, and that is a good result.

## When paying someone makes sense

Outside help is worth considering when:

- The job crosses several systems, such as an enquiry form, CRM, job tool and Xero, and errors in one break the others.
- Exceptions need clear rules and an owner, such as duplicate customers, part-payments or GST-free items.
- A mistake would affect customers or your accounts, so the workflow needs proper testing and a way to recover.
- Nobody on the team has time to build, document and maintain it.

If those apply, it may be cheaper to pay for a scoped piece of work than to keep doing the task by hand. Our [cost guide](/blog/ai-implementation-cost-australia) shows how to estimate that before committing.

## What to ask any provider

Whoever you speak to, these questions separate useful help from sales talk:

- Which specific workflow will you change, and how will we measure the result?
- Which systems will you connect, and who owns the accounts and logins afterwards?
- What happens when the automation cannot decide? Who gets the exception?
- How will you test it before it touches live customer or accounting data?
- What are the ongoing subscription and support costs?
- Where is our data processed and stored, and what do the tools' terms say about using it?
- Can we see an example of your documentation or handover?
- If we stop working with you, can our team run and change it?

Be wary of guaranteed savings, pressure to sign before anyone has looked at your process, or a proposal that never names a workflow.
